Every AI tool handles your data differently

Some train on it. Some delete it. Using the wrong tool is an ethics issue. The same product with a different plan = completely different legal framework.

How AI actually works

Inference vs. Training

Processing your prompt is not the same as updating the model. Training is a separate, expensive, deliberate industrial process.

Context Window

Each conversation is isolated. No memory between sessions unless a feature explicitly carries it forward.

Cloud Architecture

Same as email, OneDrive, any SaaS. Your prompt travels to a server, gets processed, response comes back.

Stored Communications Act

Your AI prompts are stored data (like cloud files), not stored communications (like email). Same legal category as anything else you put in cloud storage.

The 4 Tiers of Data Protection

Tier 1 — Consumer Default

Trains on your data. ChatGPT Free, Claude Free, Gemini personal. Your prompts become the product.

Tier 2 — Training Off

Toggle training off. Still governed by ToS. Better, but vendor can change terms anytime.

Tier 3 — API / DPA

Bilateral contract. No training. Data processor, not controller. Anthropic API, Reduct.

Tier 4 — Enterprise Tenancy

Data stays in a vendor-hosted tenant isolated to your organization. Enterprise agreement + DPA + admin controls. M365 Copilot, Workspace Gemini.

No tier is risk-free. Even at Tier 4, vendors retain carve-outs for legal process, safety detection, and TOS violations.

Training is the easy problem

These are the hard ones

  • Data Handling & Retention — Where does it sit, for how long, who can access it?
  • Memory Cross-Contamination — Client A's facts surfacing in Client B's conversation. Rules 1.6 + 1.7.
  • Hallucinations — Confident fabrication of case citations and facts.
  • Prompt Injection — External content manipulating model output. You get a manipulated summary.
  • Consumer Tool Seduction — Free and frictionless beats secure and inconvenient. That's where the risk lives.

Consumer AI chats are not privileged?

United States v. Heppner (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 2026)

"[T]he Court's ruling in this case appears to answer a question of first impression nationwide: whether, when a user communicates with a publicly available AI platform in connection with a pending criminal investigation, are the AI user's communications protected by attorney-client privilege or the work product doctrine?" — "simply no reasonable expectation of confidentiality" under Anthropic's Training-On consumer ToS.

"Had counsel directed Heppner to use Claude, Claude might arguably be said to have functioned in a manner akin to a highly trained professional who may act as a lawyer's agent within the protection of the attorney-client privilege."

The tier you use determines whether privilege holds

Enterprise/API tiers were explicitly distinguished. The court drew a bright line between consumer tools and contractually protected tools.

Work product: the attorney distinction

Heppner's work product claim failed on two elements

  • Not "prepared by or at the behest of counsel" — Heppner's counsel confirmed the documents "were prepared by the defendant on his own volition."
  • Did not "reflect" counsel's strategy — counsel conceded the documents "affect" strategy going forward but did not "reflect" counsel's strategy at the time.

When YOU use AI, both elements flip

If the attorney is the one using AI to analyze and prepare the case, it is prepared by counsel and reflects counsel's strategy. The work product doctrine should apply — provided you're using a tier that supports a reasonable expectation of confidentiality.

The feedback trap

When you click thumbs up or thumbs down, you give the vendor that entire conversation for training — even if you opted out.

The problem

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google retain feedback conversations for up to 5 years. Not opt-outable.

The rule

Never provide feedback on conversations with client information. The thumbs up/down button is a consent button.

Contractual gotchas

  • The Copilot Trap: Free Copilot looks identical to M365 Copilot. Same name, completely different data protections. Free version uses data for ads.
  • Meta AI: Uses conversations for ad targeting. No opt-out.
  • Gemini's Weak Opt-Out: 72-hour retention, human review possible even with activity off.
  • Feedback = consent: Thumbs up/down retains the conversation for up to 5 years.
  • Unilateral TOS changes: Consumer tools can change terms anytime without notice.
  • Memory cross-contamination: ChatGPT memory is on by default. Client A's facts can surface in Client B's chat. Turn memory off.
  • Prompt injection: External content (documents, web pages) can contain hidden instructions that manipulate AI output.

Where the states fall

47+ jurisdictions have issued guidance. They agree on the basics. They diverge on disclosure, fees, and vendor vetting.

  • Competence: must understand AI before using it (universal)
  • Verification: must verify every output (universal)
  • Confidentiality: must protect client data (universal)
  • Disclosure: SPLIT — some require, some don't
  • Fee reduction: SPLIT — Kentucky says must reduce, Virginia says no

Toggle training off — do it now

Open your phone. Follow along.

  • ChatGPT: Settings → Data Controls → "Improve the model for everyone" → OFF
  • Claude: Settings → Privacy → "Help improve Claude" → OFF
  • Gemini: myactivity.google.com → Gemini Apps → "Gemini Apps Activity" → OFF
  • Copilot (free): Settings → Privacy → "Optional Connected Experiences" → OFF

This moves you from Tier 1 to Tier 2

It's not enough for client data (that needs Tier 3+), but it stops your general research from becoming training data.

The two-stage approach

Keeps client data out of consumer tools

  • Stage 1 (Any Tier): Research the law. No client facts.
  • Stage 2 (Tier 3+ only): Draft with client facts using a DPA-protected tool.

Why this works

Stage 1 uses the best free tools for research. Stage 2 uses protected tools only when client data enters the picture.
