Research
Constitutional Issue Research
Any Tier
▶

What's OK vs. what's not

OK to include:
  • Legal questions ("What is the standard for...")
  • Statute numbers and case names
  • Jurisdictional parameters ("In Missouri...")
  • General fact patterns ("vehicle search on highway")
  • Offense types without case details
NOT OK at this tier:
  • Client names or case numbers
  • Specific facts from your case
  • County or court division (could narrow identification)
  • Officer names from your case
  • Any information from discovery

Tool comparison

ChatGPT (Free) Tier 1
Good starting point for general overviews. Ask for recent cases and then verify every citation.
Claude.ai (Free) Tier 1
Strong at nuanced analysis and longer-form research memos. Same verification requirement.
Perplexity Tier 1
Shows inline source citations automatically. Useful for quick surveys of a legal landscape. Still verify.
Gemini Deep Research Tier 1
Performs multi-step web research and produces long, sourced reports. Good for comprehensive issue surveys.
Always verify. No AI tool can be trusted to produce real case citations. Cross-reference every case with Westlaw, Lexis, Google Scholar, or Case.net before relying on it.
Deep Research with Metaprompting
Any Tier
▶

A metaprompt is a prompt that writes better prompts. Ask AI to design your research question, then use that designed prompt with Deep Research tools (Gemini, Perplexity Pro, ChatGPT Research) for dramatically better results. Full workflow on the Agents page.

Example

Naive prompt
"What are the rules for vehicle searches under the Fourth Amendment?"
Metaprompted
"Conduct exhaustive legal research on warrantless vehicle searches under the Fourth Amendment. Cover: (1) the automobile exception from Carroll v. United States through current circuit splits, (2) inventory search doctrine, (3) search incident to arrest post-Gant, (4) consent search validity factors, (5) K-9 sniff cases post-Rodriguez. For each category, identify the controlling Supreme Court case, 8th Circuit interpretation, and Missouri state court application. Organize by category with case name, year, and one-sentence holding. Flag any active circuit splits or pending cert petitions."
Verified Case Law & Legal Brushup
Any Tier
▶

AI tools hallucinate case citations. This is structural, not a bug you can prompt around. Every AI-generated citation must be verified before it touches a brief.

Verification checklist

  • Westlaw/Lexis: Run every citation. Most hallucinated cases fail here — wrong court, wrong year, or nonexistent.
  • Free alternatives: Google Scholar (scholar.google.com) and Case.net (Missouri).
  • Check good law: KeyCite or Shepard's to confirm the holding hasn't been overruled.

Compile verified cases into a reusable "brushup" — a living research memo organized by issue for your core practice areas, covering controlling cases, recent developments, and CLE resources.

Analyze Discovery for Inconsistencies
Tier 3+
▶

What's OK vs. what's not

OK with Tier 3+ tools:
  • Full discovery documents (with DPA)
  • Multiple documents for comparison
  • Asking cross-document questions
  • Requesting timelines and summaries
NEVER with Tier 1-2 tools:
  • Any discovery document, even one page
  • Witness statements with identifying details
  • Medical or forensic records
  • Copy-pasted excerpts from discovery

Workflow

  1. Upload all documents to a Tier 3+ tool. NotebookLM is ideal because it handles multiple documents natively and lets you ask questions across all of them.
  2. Ask for a timeline. "Create a chronological timeline of all events described in these documents, noting the source document for each event."
  3. Ask for contradictions. "Identify any factual inconsistencies between documents — different times, different descriptions, conflicting statements about who was present."
  4. Ask for gaps. "What events or time periods are referenced but not documented? What questions do these documents raise but not answer?"
  5. Verify and document. Every inconsistency AI identifies must be confirmed by your own reading. Document the specific page/line references for use in cross-examination preparation.

Writing with Style Guides
Build a Style Guide from Prestigious Legal Writing
Any Tier
▶

Find briefs and opinions from writers you admire. Feed them to AI. Extract the style patterns. Use those patterns as a reusable style guide.

Workflow

  1. Collect exemplar writing. Pull 3-5 briefs or opinions from writers you respect. Good sources: SCOTUS briefs (Supreme Court website), 8th Circuit opinions (Google Scholar), award-winning briefs (NACDL archives). These are all public documents — no client data.
  2. Feed them to AI. Upload or paste the documents. Prompt: "Analyze the writing style of these documents. Identify specific patterns: sentence length, paragraph structure, how arguments are introduced, transition techniques, citation integration, use of facts vs. law, tone, and rhetorical devices."
  3. Generate the style guide. Prompt: "Based on your analysis, create a concise style guide I can reuse. For each pattern, give a rule and a before/after example."
  4. Test it. Write a short argument. Then prompt: "Apply this style guide to rewrite my argument." Compare the before and after.
  5. Save and reuse. Save the style guide as a document. Upload it at the start of any writing session to maintain consistency across emails, briefs, and memos.

Apply across formats

Email (casual)
"Using my style guide, draft a quick email to co-counsel about [topic]. Keep it under 100 words, conversational but professional."
Email (professional)
"Using my style guide, draft an email to the court about [scheduling issue]. Formal tone, precise language."
Blog: 2-min Q&A
"Using my style guide, write a 2-minute read answering [legal question]. Format as Q&A. Include citation links for each authority cited."
Blog: law review style
"Using my style guide, write a 2,000-word analysis of [issue]. Use footnotes (not endnotes). Each footnote should include a hyperlink to the source."

Drafting
Draft a Motion to Suppress
Tier 3+
▶

What's OK vs. what's not

OK at any tier (Stage 1 research):
  • General legal standards
  • "Vehicle search Fourth Amendment Missouri"
  • Case law research with no client facts
  • Template motion structures
Tier 3+ only (Stage 2 drafting):
  • Client's name, DOB, case number
  • What was found, where, when
  • Officer actions and statements
  • Any facts from the police report

Two-stage approach

Stage 1 (Any Tier): Research the law. "What are the current standards for challenging a vehicle search in Missouri under the Fourth Amendment? Include controlling 8th Circuit and Missouri appellate cases." No client data needed.
Stage 2 (Tier 3+ only): Draft the motion with facts. Upload the police report to a Tier 3+ tool. "Using this police report and the legal standards from [Stage 1 research], draft a motion to suppress the evidence seized during the vehicle search."

Tool comparison for Stage 2

Claude API Tier 3
Strong at legal writing. Upload police report + paste research. Zero data retention with DPA. Good first-draft quality.
M365 Copilot Tier 4
Draft directly in Word. References documents in your SharePoint/OneDrive. Stays within your organization's tenant.
AI drafts, you file. Every motion must be reviewed for factual accuracy, legal accuracy, jurisdictional fit, and local rule compliance. AI-generated motions are first drafts, not finished products. You are responsible for everything you file. (See Heppner — consumer AI chat logs are discoverable. Full case breakdown.)
Draft a Sentencing Memo with Mitigation Research
Tier 3+
▶

Two-stage approach

Stage 1 — Mitigation research (Any Tier): Research sentencing factors, statutory ranges, and mitigation strategies without using client-specific facts. "What mitigation factors does Missouri recognize in sentencing for [offense type]? What are the statutory ranges? What programs or alternatives to incarceration exist?"
Stage 2 — Drafting (Tier 3+ only): Upload PSI, social history, treatment records (all High sensitivity). "Draft a sentencing memorandum using the mitigation factors identified in [Stage 1] and the specific facts from these documents."

Mitigation research prompts (safe at any tier)

  • "What does current neuroscience research say about brain development and decision-making in 18-25 year olds? Cite peer-reviewed sources."
  • "What are the recidivism outcomes for [program type] compared to incarceration for [offense type]? Cite studies."
  • "What sentencing alternatives has Missouri adopted in the last 5 years for [offense category]?"
Client Correspondence
Tier 3+
▶

The ethical line

If the letter mentions the client's name, case number, charges, or case-specific facts — it's High sensitivity data. Use Tier 3+ tools only.

Safer alternatives

  • Template approach (Any Tier): Ask AI to draft a letter template with placeholders: "[CLIENT NAME]", "[CHARGE]", "[NEXT COURT DATE]". Then fill in the specifics yourself in Word.
  • De-identified approach (Tier 2+): "Draft a letter explaining a plea offer of [X years] for a [charge type] to a client with no criminal history. Explain the pros and cons in plain language." No identifiable data used.
  • Full draft (Tier 3+ only): Upload the plea offer and draft a personalized letter. Requires DPA-protected tool.
Transcription & Audio
Jail Call Transcription
Tier 3+
▶

What's OK vs. what's not

OK with Tier 3+ tools:
  • Full audio file (with DPA in place)
  • Transcription and summarization
  • Searching across multiple calls
  • Identifying key statements
NEVER with Tier 1-2 tools:
  • Any jail call audio, even a clip
  • Transcripts from calls
  • Client voiceprints (biometric data)
  • Family members' voices and names

Tool comparison

Reduct Tier 3
Purpose-built for legal audio. SOC 2 compliant. Transcription + AI summarization + redaction tools. MSPD has an enterprise agreement. Recommended for MSPD attorneys.
Microsoft Word Transcribe Tier 4
Built into Word (M365). Processes within your tenant. Limited to 300 minutes/month. Significant limitations: no native video support — you must convert video to audio first, then upload one file at a time. The editing pane is minimal: once you commit the transcript to Word, you lose the timestamped editing interface. Accuracy drops significantly with poor audio quality (jail phones, background noise). Workable for short, clear recordings but painful for batch processing.
Otter.ai Tier 1
Do not use for client audio. Consumer tool. Data may be used for model training. No DPA available for individual users. Popular but ethically unsuitable for privileged recordings.
Body Camera Footage Analysis
Tier 3+
▶

The use case

BWC footage can be hours long. AI transcription and summarization can help you find the critical moments — the Miranda warnings, the consent question, the use of force — without watching every minute.

Tool comparison

Reduct Tier 3
Best option for video. Accepts video files directly — no conversion to audio required. Searchable transcripts linked to video timestamps. Click any word to jump to that moment. Redaction tools for preparing exhibits. AI summarization. MSPD has an enterprise agreement. Recommended for MSPD attorneys.
JusticeText Tier 3
Built specifically for defense attorneys working with video evidence. Transcription, timestamped clips, annotation. CJIS compliant.
Microsoft Word Transcribe Tier 4
Not designed for video. You must extract audio from BWC video, then upload one file at a time (300 min/month limit). No timestamped video linking. Workable in a pinch but impractical for multi-hour BWC footage.
MSPD attorneys: Reduct is your best path. It handles video natively, links transcripts to timestamps, and MSPD already has the enterprise agreement in place. Word Transcribe works but requires video-to-audio conversion and one-at-a-time uploads.
NotebookLM Podcast Generation
Any Tier
▶

NotebookLM generates a podcast-style audio briefing from uploaded documents. Two AI hosts discuss the material conversationally. Safe at any tier when using public documents (ethics opinions, bar guidance, court rules).

How to do it

  1. Open NotebookLM (notebooklm.google.com). Create a new notebook.
  2. Add sources. Paste URLs from the ethics opinions URL list or upload PDFs. Accepts up to 50 sources.
  3. Customize focus. Add a note: "Focus on how Missouri's opinion compares to the ABA and what vendor vetting steps Missouri requires."
  4. Generate. Click "Generate" on Audio Overview. Creates a ~10-15 minute podcast.

Case Strategy
Jury Selection Brainstorming
Any Tier
▶

What's OK vs. what's not

OK at any tier:
  • General voir dire questions by case type
  • Social science research on juror attitudes
  • Questionnaire templates
  • Bias identification strategies
Tier 3+ only:
  • Actual juror names or panel lists
  • Case-specific facts for targeted questions
  • Specific venue/county information
  • Co-defendant or victim identities

Example prompts

  • "What voir dire questions are most effective for identifying juror bias in DWI cases? Include questions from published trial advocacy resources."
  • "Generate a list of supplemental juror questionnaire questions for a case involving allegations of self-defense. Focus on attitudes about gun ownership, use of force, and stand-your-ground principles."
  • "What does the social science research say about juror attitudes toward [issue]? Cite peer-reviewed studies."
The line: "What questions should I ask in a DWI voir dire?" is general research (any tier). "My client John Smith is charged with DWI in Cole County and the arresting officer is..." is case-specific (Tier 3+ only).
De-Identified Case Strategy Analysis
Tier 2+
▶

Replace all identifying information with generic descriptions. Use "my client" not the name, "a mid-Missouri county" not the specific county, "Class D felony drug possession" not the case number. Remove DOB, SSN, booking numbers.

Re-identification risk. If the facts are distinctive enough that someone could Google them and find your client, treat it as High sensitivity regardless of de-identification. See the Risk Matrix.

Example

Not de-identified
"John Smith was arrested in Boone County on Jan 15, 2026 for possession of methamphetamine after a traffic stop on I-70. Case no. 26BA-CR00123."
De-identified
"My client was arrested in a mid-Missouri county for possession of a controlled substance after a highway traffic stop. What are the strongest suppression arguments based on these facts: [describe the stop circumstances without identifying details]."
Sentencing Mitigation Research
Any Tier
▶

Mitigation research — neuroscience, recidivism data, treatment programs, sentencing alternatives — involves no client-specific facts. Use metaprompting + Deep Research for comprehensive results.

Research categories

  • Brain development, trauma, substance use disorder as medical condition
  • Treatment vs. incarceration outcomes by offense type
  • Drug courts, veterans courts, mental health courts, diversion programs
  • Comparative sentencing in your jurisdiction and nationally

Remember: The tier system is about where your data goes, not how good the AI is. A free tool used for general research (Tier 1, Low sensitivity) can be perfectly ethical. An expensive enterprise tool used to draft a motion (Tier 4, High sensitivity) is the safest option. Match the tool's protection level to your data's sensitivity level.
