What's an agent? An AI "agent" is a workflow where AI performs multiple steps automatically — reading, deciding, and acting — instead of just answering a single question. Think of it as the difference between asking a question and delegating a task. The same Risk Matrix rules apply: match the data sensitivity to the tool's protection tier.
Metaprompting
Metaprompting: Write Prompts That Write Better Prompts
Any Tier
▶

Instead of crafting the perfect question yourself, ask AI to design the question for you. Then use that designed prompt to get dramatically better results from Deep Research tools.

Template

"I need to research [legal issue]. Write me a detailed research prompt that will produce the most thorough results. Specify: exact scope, jurisdictions, source types, output format, and what to do when sources conflict."

Example

Naive
"Tell me about Fourth Amendment vehicle searches."
Metaprompted
"Conduct exhaustive legal research on warrantless vehicle searches under the Fourth Amendment. Cover: (1) automobile exception from Carroll through current circuit splits, (2) inventory search doctrine, (3) search incident to arrest post-Gant, (4) consent search validity factors, (5) K-9 sniff cases post-Rodriguez. For each: controlling Supreme Court case, 8th Circuit interpretation, Missouri state application. Organize by category with case name, year, one-sentence holding. Flag active circuit splits or pending cert."

Paste the metaprompted result into Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity Pro, or ChatGPT Research. These tools perform multi-step browsing and produce comprehensive reports. See Use Cases for the full workflow.

Workflow Automation
Creating a Zapier or Power Automate Flow
Tier 3+
▶

Zapier and Power Automate connect apps: when something happens in one app (trigger), it does something in another (action). The ethical question: a Zapier flow that processes case information sends data through Zapier's servers.

Where data goes

  • Does Zapier/Power Automate have a DPA? Zapier offers a DPA for paid plans. Power Automate inherits your M365 data protections.
  • What AI model does the "AI step" use? Zapier's built-in AI uses OpenAI. If your flow includes an AI processing step, client data goes to OpenAI's API (Tier 3 with DPA).
  • Where is data stored? Zapier retains task history. Power Automate stores within your M365 tenant.
Power Automate is safer for legal workflows because everything stays within your Microsoft 365 tenant. Zapier is more flexible but introduces a third-party data processor. For flows that touch client data, prefer Power Automate.

Safe starter flows (no client data)

  • CLE tracking: When you complete a CLE, log it to a spreadsheet automatically
  • Legal news: When a legal news RSS feed has a new item matching your keywords, save it to a reading list
  • Deadline reminders: 7 days before a calendar event tagged "court," send yourself a reminder email with a checklist
Scheduled Tasks and Recurring Events
Varies
▶

The concept

Some AI tasks should happen automatically on a schedule, not just when you remember to do them. Examples:

Recurring research updates
Every Monday, check for new 8th Circuit opinions on [your practice areas]. Summarize any new developments. Any Tier — public case law, no client data.
Deadline context reminders
3 days before each court date, pull the case summary from your CMS and email you a brief: what the hearing is about, what's pending, what to prepare. Tier 4 — touches case data.
Weekly ethics opinion check
Every Friday, check the NYC Bar tracker and ABA for new AI ethics opinions. If any are new since last check, summarize and email you. Any Tier — public documents.

How to set these up

  • Power Automate: Use "Recurrence" trigger to run flows on a schedule. Connect to Outlook, SharePoint, and Copilot.
  • Zapier: Use "Schedule" trigger. Connect to Gmail, Google Sheets, and AI steps.
  • Google Apps Script: Free, runs on a timer, works with Gmail and Google Sheets. Requires some scripting but AI can write the script for you.

Meetings, Calendar & Transcription
Which Meeting Tools Are Safe for AI Features?
Varies
▶

The problem

Every major meeting platform now offers AI features: meeting summaries, action items, transcription. These features listen to your entire meeting. If you're discussing client matters, that audio — and the AI-generated summary — is privileged and confidential data. Where does it go?

Meeting tool comparison

Zoom AI Companion Tier 3
Does not train on meeting content. After 2023 backlash, Zoom explicitly updated TOS: "Zoom does not use any customer audio, video, chat, or other communications-like content to train AI models." DPA available for Business/Enterprise. ZDR option available — deletes temporary transcripts immediately after summary creation. AI Companion is off by default; admin must enable. Participants see an in-meeting indicator. Retains data up to 30 days for support unless ZDR is enabled. Source
Cisco Webex AI Assistant Tier 3
Does not retain data after processing. Uses Azure OpenAI as AI provider; Microsoft contractually commits not to use customer content for model training and does not store Cisco customer data. DPA available. SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, FedRAMP Authorized, HIPAA BAA available. Feature-specific retention policies at Cisco Trust Portal. Source
Microsoft Teams + Copilot Tier 4
Safest option. All processing within your M365 tenant. Meeting summaries, transcripts, and action items stay in your organization's data boundary. No training on customer content. Requires M365 E3/E5 + Copilot license.
Google Meet + Gemini Tier 4
Safe with Workspace. Even Business Starter ($7/mo) gets enterprise data protections. Meeting notes and summaries processed within your Workspace tenant. Not used for training.

Third-party AI notetakers

Granola Tier 2
Trains on anonymized data by default — users can opt out in Settings. Enterprise plans have training off by default. SOC 2 Type 2 (July 2025). No audio stored — only transcripts. Uses OpenAI and Anthropic for summarization. DPA available on request. No bot added to calls. Source
Otter.ai Tier 1
Do not use for client meetings. Consumer tool with AI training on user data. No meaningful DPA for individual accounts. Popular but ethically unsuitable for privileged communications.
Fireflies.ai Tier 1
Do not use for client meetings. Joins meetings as a bot, records everything, sends to cloud for processing. Consumer-grade privacy controls.
The Otter/Fireflies trap. These tools are popular because they're easy. But "easy" and "ethical" are different questions. If a colleague's Otter bot joins your client strategy call, that's your client's privileged communications going to a consumer AI platform. Raise this at your next team meeting.

Lessons from Healthcare: AI Ambient Listening
How Doctors Solved the Same Problem (HIPAA & BAAs)
Reference
▶

The parallel

Doctors face the exact same problem lawyers do: they need AI to help with documentation, but their conversations contain protected information. Healthcare solved this through HIPAA and Business Associate Agreements (BAAs). There are direct lessons for legal practice.

What doctors are using

DAX Copilot (Microsoft/Nuance)
AI "ambient listener" that sits in the exam room, listens to the doctor-patient conversation, and auto-generates clinical notes. HIPAA compliant, BAA with Microsoft, runs on Azure with regional data residency. Doctor reviews and signs off on AI-generated notes in the EHR. Audio processed via HIPAA-compliant methods.
Abridge
Ambient AI scribe. HIPAA compliant, enterprise-grade with SSO and granular governance controls. Currently facing a class action lawsuit (Saucedo v. Sharp HealthCare, San Diego Superior Court, Nov 2025) alleging recordings without proper patient consent.
Suki
Voice-first AI scribe. HIPAA compliant, BAA available. Designed for real-time note generation during patient encounters.

BAA vs. DPA: the same idea, different professions

Healthcare: BAA
A Business Associate Agreement is required by HIPAA whenever a vendor processes Protected Health Information (PHI). It's legally mandated — no BAA, no PHI processing, period. Specifies: what data is processed, how it's protected, breach notification requirements, vendor liability.
Legal: DPA
A Data Processing Agreement serves the same function for attorney-client data. Unlike HIPAA's BAA, there's no single statute mandating DPAs for legal data. Instead, it flows from Rule 1.6 (confidentiality), bar ethics opinions, and GDPR (for EU data). The obligation is the same — the enforcement mechanism is weaker.
The healthcare lesson for lawyers: Doctors don't debate whether to use a BAA with their AI scribe — HIPAA makes it non-negotiable. Lawyers should treat DPAs the same way. If an AI vendor won't give you a DPA or equivalent contract for data processing, that vendor is not appropriate for client data. Full stop.

Emerging problems (same risks for lawyers)

  • Consent for recording: 13 states require all-party consent for recordings. If your AI tool is "listening" to a client meeting, you need consent. California AB 3030 (Jan 2025) now requires AI disclosure in healthcare — similar rules will come for legal services.
  • AI hallucinations in records: AI scribes can fabricate or distort content. Doctors must review AI-generated notes before signing off. Lawyers must review AI-generated documents before filing. Same risk, same obligation.
  • Consent theater: In the Abridge lawsuit, the EHR automatically inserted boilerplate language saying patients had "consented" when no actual consent conversation occurred. Lawyers should ensure their AI consent processes are genuine, not auto-generated checkboxes.

Missouri recording law

Missouri is a one-party consent state for recording (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 542.402). You can record a conversation you're a party to without the other party's knowledge. However, ethical best practice is to inform clients that an AI tool is listening and get their consent, regardless of what the wiretapping statute allows. ABA Opinion 512 and Missouri Opinion 2024-11 both flow from informed consent principles.

Why the tier depends on how you log in

Claude Code supports two authentication methods, and which one you use determines your data protection tier:

Consumer login (Pro/Max) Tier 1
Governed by Consumer Terms. Training on by default. Your code and prompts may be used to train future models. Data retained up to 5 years (de-identified). Toggle off in Settings → "Help improve Claude" to reach Tier 2 (30-day retention, no training).
API key (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY) Tier 3

For client-related coding work, authenticate with an API key (Tier 3) or use an Enterprise plan (Tier 4). The same code, the same tool — completely different legal framework depending on which auth method you use.

The pattern: Every agent and automation workflow comes back to the same question — what data does the AI touch? If it touches client data, use Tier 3+ tools. If it touches only public information, any tier is fine. Automation makes you faster, but it doesn't change the ethical rules.
