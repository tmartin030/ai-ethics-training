How to use these prompts

Each prompt is ready to copy and paste. Customize the [bracketed text] for your situation.

  • Any Tier = no client data involved. Use ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity — whatever you prefer.
  • Tier 3+ = contains or touches client data. Use only DPA-protected tools (Claude API, NotebookLM, M365 Copilot, Reduct).
  • Always verify AI-generated case citations on Westlaw, Lexis, or Google Scholar before relying on them.
Research
Metaprompt: Write a Better Research Prompt
Any Tier
▶

Instead of asking your question directly, ask AI to design the question for you. Then use that designed prompt with Deep Research tools for dramatically better results.

I need to research [legal issue]. Write me a detailed research prompt that will produce the most thorough results. Specify: exact scope, relevant jurisdictions [8th Circuit + Missouri / your jurisdiction], source types to consult, preferred output format, and what to do when sources conflict.
Customize: [legal issue] = your research topic. [jurisdiction] = your circuit + state.
Pro tip: Paste the metaprompted result into Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity Pro, or ChatGPT Research for a comprehensive, multi-source report.
Fourth Amendment Vehicle Search Survey
Any Tier
▶
Conduct exhaustive legal research on warrantless vehicle searches under the Fourth Amendment. Cover: (1) automobile exception from Carroll v. United States through current circuit splits, (2) inventory search doctrine, (3) search incident to arrest post-Gant, (4) consent search validity factors, (5) K-9 sniff cases post-Rodriguez. For each category, identify the controlling Supreme Court case, [8th Circuit] interpretation, and [Missouri] state court application. Organize by category with case name, year, and one-sentence holding. Flag any active circuit splits or pending cert petitions.
Customize: [8th Circuit] and [Missouri] = your circuit and state.
Pro tip: This is already a metaprompted result. Paste directly into Deep Research.
Case Law Survey by Issue Area
Any Tier
▶
Survey all [8th Circuit and Missouri] case law on [legal issue] from the last [5] years. For each case: case name, citation, year, court, one-sentence holding, and whether it's still good law. Organize chronologically. Note any trend in how courts are moving on this issue. Identify the strongest and weakest authority for [defense / prosecution].
Customize: [legal issue], [jurisdiction], [time period], [defense / prosecution].
Sentencing Mitigation Research
Any Tier
▶
Research sentencing mitigation for [offense type] cases. Cover: (1) what mitigation factors does [Missouri] recognize for this offense category, (2) statutory sentencing range and any mandatory minimums, (3) available alternatives to incarceration (drug courts, diversion programs, probation options), (4) relevant social science on recidivism outcomes for treatment vs. incarceration, (5) any recent legislative changes to sentencing in this area. Cite peer-reviewed studies and statutes.
Customize: [offense type] = drug possession, DWI, assault, etc. [Missouri] = your state.
Legislative History / Statutory Interpretation
Any Tier
▶
Trace the legislative history of [statute citation, e.g., Mo. Rev. Stat. § 571.030]. Identify: (1) when the statute was originally enacted and its original language, (2) every amendment with year, bill number, and what changed, (3) any committee reports or floor debate transcripts discussing legislative intent, (4) how [Missouri] courts have interpreted ambiguous terms, (5) any pending legislation that would modify this statute.
Customize: [statute citation] = the specific section you're researching.
Pro tip: Cross-reference AI results with the official Missouri Revisor of Statutes site (revisor.mo.gov) for current text and history notes.
Drafting
Motion to Suppress — Stage 1 (Research)
Any Tier
▶
What are the current legal standards for challenging a [vehicle search / traffic stop / warrantless entry / consent search] in [Missouri] under the Fourth Amendment? Include controlling 8th Circuit and Missouri appellate cases. For each standard, provide: (1) the legal test, (2) the leading case, (3) how Missouri courts have applied it, (4) common factual patterns where suppression was granted vs. denied.
Customize: [type of search/seizure] and [jurisdiction].
No client data in this prompt. This is pure legal research — safe at any tier. Save the output for Stage 2.
Motion to Suppress — Stage 2 (Drafting)
Tier 3+
▶
Using the attached police report and the following legal research, draft a motion to suppress the evidence seized during the [vehicle search / traffic stop / etc.]. Structure: (1) statement of facts drawn from the police report, (2) legal standards from the research, (3) argument applying the standards to these specific facts, (4) conclusion and prayer for relief. Use Missouri motion formatting. Flag any facts in the report that support the motion and any facts that cut against it.
Attach: police report + Stage 1 research output.
This prompt requires client data. Only use with Tier 3+ tools (Claude API, M365 Copilot). Never paste client facts into ChatGPT Free, Claude Free, or Gemini personal.

Client Letter — Plea Offer Explanation
Any Tier (template) / Tier 3+ (full draft)
▶

Template approach (Any Tier):

Draft a letter template explaining a plea offer to a client. The offer is [X years / probation / SIS / SES] for a [charge type]. The client has [no criminal history / prior convictions for X]. Use placeholders: [CLIENT NAME], [CASE NUMBER], [NEXT COURT DATE]. Explain in plain language: (1) what the offer means, (2) what happens if they accept, (3) what happens if they reject and go to trial, (4) sentencing range if convicted at trial. No legalese. Write at a 9th-grade reading level.
Customize: [offer terms], [charge type], [criminal history]. Fill in placeholders yourself in Word.
Pro tip: The template approach keeps client data out of AI entirely. You fill in the names and case numbers after.
Document Analysis
Police Report Summary
Tier 3+
▶
Summarize the attached police report. Provide: (1) a one-paragraph narrative summary, (2) a chronological timeline of events with times, (3) all individuals mentioned with their roles (officer, witness, suspect, victim), (4) all physical evidence collected, (5) any Miranda warnings given (and when), (6) any statements attributed to anyone. Flag anything that seems inconsistent, unusual, or legally significant.
Discovery Inconsistency Analysis
Tier 3+
▶
I've uploaded [X] documents from discovery in a criminal case. Do the following: 1. Create a chronological timeline of all events described across all documents. Note the source document for each event. 2. Identify any factual inconsistencies between documents — different times, different descriptions, conflicting statements about who was present. 3. Identify any gaps — events or time periods that are referenced but not documented. 4. For each inconsistency or gap, explain why it might matter for the defense. 5. List any questions these documents raise but don't answer.
Upload: police reports, witness statements, lab results, probable cause statement, etc.
Pro tip: NotebookLM handles multiple documents natively. Upload all at once and ask questions across the entire set.
Multi-Document Comparison
Tier 3+
▶
Compare the officer's description of [the stop / the search / the arrest] in the probable cause statement with the description in the police report [and the body camera transcript, if available]. For each factual claim, note: (1) what each document says, (2) whether they agree or disagree, (3) if they disagree, which version is more favorable for the defense and why.
Timeline Extraction
Tier 3+
▶
Extract a detailed chronological timeline from the attached documents. For each event: (1) exact date and time (if available), (2) what happened, (3) who was involved, (4) which document is the source (with page number if possible). Organize in a table format. Highlight any time gaps longer than [30 minutes] that are not accounted for.
PSI Review and Fact-Check
Tier 3+
▶
Review the attached PSI (Pre-Sentence Investigation Report). Identify: (1) any factual claims that are not supported by the underlying discovery documents (also attached), (2) any characterizations of the defendant that are editorializing rather than fact-reporting, (3) any sentencing-relevant information that is missing (employment, education, family support, mental health, substance abuse treatment), (4) any errors in criminal history or charges. Create a list of objections organized by PSI section number.
Upload: PSI + discovery documents for cross-reference.
Voir Dire & Trial Prep
Voir Dire Question Generation
Any Tier
▶
Generate voir dire questions for a [DWI / drug possession / assault / domestic violence / homicide] case. For each question: (1) what bias or attitude it's designed to surface, (2) whether it's a for-cause or peremptory challenge question, (3) what answer would be favorable vs. unfavorable for the defense. Include questions from published trial advocacy resources. Organize by topic: attitudes toward [law enforcement / the charge type / the defendant's background / reasonable doubt].
Customize: [case type] and [specific topics] relevant to your case.
Supplemental Juror Questionnaire
Any Tier
▶
Draft a supplemental juror questionnaire for a [case type] trial. Include [20-30] questions covering: (1) media exposure to the case or similar cases, (2) personal experiences with [law enforcement / the criminal justice system / the specific crime type], (3) attitudes about [self-defense / drug use / mental health / police credibility], (4) ability to follow the law on [burden of proof / presumption of innocence / specific jury instructions]. Use a mix of yes/no, scale (1-5), and short-answer questions. Keep language accessible.
Cross-Examination Prep from Depositions
Tier 3+
▶
Review the attached [deposition transcript / prior testimony / police report] for [witness name / officer name]. Identify: (1) every factual claim the witness makes, (2) any internal inconsistencies within the testimony, (3) any statements that contradict the other discovery documents, (4) any areas where the witness hedges, qualifies, or is uncertain, (5) any assumptions the witness makes that could be challenged. For each point, draft a leading cross-examination question.
Jury Instruction Analysis
Any Tier
▶
Analyze the pattern jury instructions for [MAI-CR 3d § XX.XX / your state's instruction] for a [charge type] case. Break down: (1) each element the prosecution must prove, (2) what evidence would satisfy each element, (3) what evidence would negate each element, (4) any lesser-included offenses and their instructions, (5) any defense instructions that might apply (self-defense, intoxication, mistake of fact, etc.)]. For each element, explain in plain language what the jury will be asked to decide.
Customize: [instruction number] and [charge type]. MAI-CR 3d = Missouri Approved Instructions.
Writing & Editing
Extract a Style Guide from Exemplar Writing
Any Tier
▶

Upload 3-5 briefs or opinions from writers you admire (public documents only).

Analyze the writing style of these documents. Identify specific patterns: sentence length and variation, paragraph structure, how arguments are introduced and transitioned, citation integration (inline vs. footnotes), use of facts vs. law, tone, and rhetorical devices. Based on your analysis, create a concise, reusable style guide. For each pattern, give a rule and a before/after example.
Pro tip: Save the generated style guide as a document. Upload it at the start of any writing session: "Apply this style guide to the following draft."
Polish / Edit a Draft
Any Tier (no client data) / Tier 3+ (with client data)
▶
Review this draft for: (1) passive voice — convert to active, (2) unnecessary qualifiers — "very," "somewhat," "rather," "quite," (3) sentences over 30 words — split or tighten, (4) paragraphs over 5 sentences — break up, (5) throat-clearing — weak openings like "It is important to note that." For each issue, show the original and your suggested edit.
Tone Shift (Formal to Conversational or Vice Versa)
Any Tier
▶

Formal to conversational:

Rewrite this text for a general audience. Keep the substance but make it conversational: shorter sentences, no legalese, active voice, plain language. Aim for a 9th-grade reading level. If a legal term is essential, define it in parentheses the first time it appears.

Conversational to formal:

Rewrite this text for a court filing. Use formal legal register: precise terminology, citation format, structured paragraphs with topic sentences. Remove colloquialisms, contractions, and first person. Maintain the substantive arguments.
Citation Formatting
Any Tier
▶
Review the citations in this document for Bluebook formatting errors. For each citation: (1) identify what's wrong, (2) provide the corrected citation. Check: case name italicization, reporter abbreviations, pinpoint citations, subsequent history, and signal usage (see, see also, cf., etc.). Flag any citations that appear to be hallucinated (unusual reporter, wrong court for the reporter, year doesn't match).
Caution: AI is mediocre at Bluebook formatting. Use this as a first pass, not a final check. Always verify citations independently.
Transcription & Audio
Jail Call Summary
Tier 3+
▶
Transcribe and summarize this jail call recording. Provide: (1) a one-paragraph summary of the conversation, (2) all participants identified (by voice if names aren't stated), (3) any statements that could be relevant to the case — admissions, plans, references to the charges or co-defendants, (4) any statements that indicate the caller's mental state, (5) timestamps for each significant statement.
Pro tip: Use Reduct for jail calls. It links transcripts to audio timestamps — click any word to hear that moment.
BWC Key-Moment Extraction
Tier 3+
▶
Review the transcript of this body camera footage. Identify and timestamp: (1) initial contact — what was said and how the stop was initiated, (2) any Miranda warnings (exact wording and time), (3) any consent requests and responses, (4) any use of force or physical contact, (5) any statements by the suspect, (6) any statements by witnesses, (7) when backup arrived, (8) when the suspect was placed in custody. For each moment, note the exact timestamp and quote the relevant language.
Multi-Call Pattern Analysis
Tier 3+
▶
I've uploaded transcripts from [X] jail calls. Analyze across all calls for: (1) who the caller spoke with most frequently, (2) recurring topics or themes, (3) any evolution in the caller's account of events over time, (4) any mentions of co-defendants, witnesses, or evidence, (5) any statements about the attorney-client relationship or case strategy (flag these — they may be privileged). Create a summary table: date, duration, participants, key topics, notable statements.
