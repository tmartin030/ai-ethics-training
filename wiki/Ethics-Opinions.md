# Ethics Opinions & Guidance on AI

Every formal and informal ethics opinion, guidance document, court policy, and key case addressing lawyer use of AI that we could find as of March 2026.

## ABA

- **[Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf)** (July 2024) -- First comprehensive AI ethics opinion. Duties of competence, confidentiality, communication, candor, supervision, and reasonable fees all apply. Must verify outputs. Must obtain informed consent before inputting client data into tools that train on input.

## U.S. States

- **Alaska -- [Ethics Opinion 2025-1](https://alaskabar.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-1.pdf)** (2025) -- Must understand technology, review all outputs, anonymize prompts.
- **Arizona -- [Best Practices Guidance](https://www.azbar.org/for-legal-professionals/practice-tools-management/practice-2-0/choosing-and-using-technology/best-practices-for-using-artificial-intelligence/)** -- Guidance, not formal opinion.
- **California -- [COPRAC Practical Guidance](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/ethics/Generative-AI-Practical-Guidance.pdf)** (Nov 2023) -- First state-level AI guidance. Never input confidential info without protections.
- **DC -- [Ethics Opinion 388](https://www.dcbar.org/for-lawyers/legal-ethics/ethics-opinions-210-present/ethics-opinion-388)** (April 2024) -- Must protect confidentiality especially with free tools that exploit inputs.
- **Florida -- [Advisory Opinion 24-1](https://www.floridabar.org/etopinions/opinion-24-1/)** (Jan 2024) -- No double-billing; ensure chatbot disclosure to potential clients.
- **Kentucky -- [Ethics Opinion E-457](https://kybar.org/page/opinsethics#2024)** (March 2024) -- Failing to use available AI *may constitute* a competence failure. Fees must be reduced.
- **Michigan -- [Advisory Opinion JI-155](https://www.michbar.org/opinions/ethics/numbered_opinions/JI-155)** (2023) -- Technological competence obligation.
- **Mississippi -- [Formal Opinion 267](https://www.msbar.org/ethics-discipline/ethics-opinions/formal-opinions/267/)** (Nov 2024) -- Informed consent for AI use.
- **Missouri -- [Informal Opinion 2024-11](https://mo-legal-ethics.org/informal-opinion/2024-11/)** (April 2024) -- **Your jurisdiction.** Must assess vendor terms, data security, input usage. See [[Risk-Matrix]] for how this maps to the tier framework.
- **New Jersey -- [Supreme Court Guidelines](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/notices/2024/01/n240125a.pdf)** (Jan 2024) -- AI can hallucinate; verify everything.
- **New Mexico -- [Opinion 2024-004](https://www.sbnm.org/Portals/NMBAR/GenAI%20Formal%20Opinion%20-%20Sept_2024_FINAL.pdf)** (Sept 2024) -- Firm-wide AI policies required.
- **NYC Bar -- [Opinion 2024-5](https://www.nycbar.org/reports/formal-opinion-2024-5-generative-ai-in-the-practice-of-law/)** (Aug 2024) -- Guardrails, not hard restrictions. [Opinion 2025-6](https://www.nycbar.org/reports/formal-opinion-2025-6-ethical-issues-affecting-use-of-ai-to-record-transcribe-and-summarize-conversations-with-clients/) (Dec 2025) -- Client consent required for AI recording/transcription.
- **NYSBA -- [Task Force Report](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2024-April-Report-and-Recommendations-of-the-Task-Force-on-Artificial-Intelligence.pdf)** (April 2024); [AI & Access to Justice](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Revised-01.20.2026-Approved-Report-and-Recommendations-on-AI-and-Access-to-Justice-in-2025-online-version.pdf) (Jan 2026)
- **North Carolina -- [Opinion 2024-1](https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/ethics/adopted-opinions/2024-formal-ethics-opinion-1/)** (2024) -- Must evaluate vendor security protocols.
- **Oregon -- [Opinion 2025-205](https://www.osbar.org/_docs/ethics/2025-205.pdf)** (2025) -- Verify every citation; firm-wide AI policies required.
- **Pennsylvania -- [Joint Opinion 2024-200](https://www.lawnext.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Joint-Formal-Opinion-2024-200.pdf)** (May 2024) -- Joint PA + Philly bar opinion.
- **Texas -- [Opinion 705](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/opinions/opinion-705/)** (Feb 2025) -- May pass through subscription costs with client agreement.
- **Virginia -- [Opinion 1901](https://vsb.org/Site/Site/news/rules-news/20250327-prop-1901-fees-ai.aspx)** (Nov 2025) -- Most permissive: no fee reduction requirement, no blanket disclosure.

## Comparison: Through-Lines & Divergences

**Universal agreement:**
- Competence: must understand AI before using it
- Confidentiality: must protect client data
- Verification: must verify every output -- no exceptions anywhere
- Supervision: must supervise non-lawyer AI use

**Where opinions split:**
- Client disclosure (Virginia: no requirement vs. ABA: informed consent for training tools)
- Fee reduction (Kentucky: must reduce vs. Virginia: no requirement)
- Vendor vetting (ABA/Missouri: specific requirements vs. Virginia/Michigan: silent)
- Firm policies (Oregon/New Mexico: required vs. most states: silent)
- Affirmative duty (Kentucky alone: not using AI may be a competence failure)

**Missouri sits in the moderate middle.** When in doubt, follow ABA 512 as the floor.
